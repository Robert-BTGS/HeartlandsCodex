The Primordials

Before the Gods:

Before gods, before mortals, before worlds—there existed the Primordials. These were not gods nor beings, but raw cosmic principles, pure forces of reality itself. Their presence was so immense and alien that naming them with mortal tongues bordered on futility. Yet mortals, ever curious, named the first and greatest of these forces Oris, the embodiment of pure existence, endlessly wandering the infinite Void.

In its solitary eternity, Oris grew lonely and began to fracture. From these fractures arose two sentient entities: Vaoris and Eloris. With their birth, Oris faded back into the Void itself, leaving the two beings alone. Vaoris, embodying change and chaos, yearned for transformation, progression, and expression. Eloris, embodying order and stability, desired permanence, structure, and tranquility. Their clashes sparked new ideas, consciousness, and entities, filling the void around them with complex constructs and ethereal thoughts.

Eventually, their struggles gave rise to countless sentient forms known as The Dren, beings of pure thought without physical shape. Seeking coherence, Vaoris and Eloris constructed a new realm called Oravyn, a sanctuary distinct from the Void, forcing the Dren into tangible forms. Having fulfilled their roles, Vaoris and Eloris withdrew silently into the Void, leaving Oravyn to its new inhabitants.

Initially furious at their entrapment, the Dren erupted into endless conflict, tearing each other apart only to recombine endlessly due to their immortality. Their existence became marked by eternal cycles of destruction and rebirth.

After eons of pointless war, a realization struck the Dren: their only escape from physical form lay in constructing new realms within Oravyn and pouring their essence into these creations, shedding physicality entirely. Divided by differing visions, the Dren split into two factions: the Didren and the Adren.

The Didren pursued individualistic visions, creating countless separate realms collectively known as Mythara, each reflecting their personal desires, ambitions, and vanity. In contrast, the Adren unified their efforts into one grand realm called Erasyl, populating it with mortal life to house their essences and provide a harmonious existence. The Didren, envious of the Adren's collective achievement, repeatedly assaulted Erasyl, but the Adren defended and rebuilt tirelessly.

At a crucial turning point, the Adren discovered how to strip immortality away when a powerful Didren named Orathis attacked Erasyl. They succeeded in killing him, scattering his essence throughout Oravyn, the largest chunk becoming the moon Thirel. This act created pure magical energy and Heart Shards—fragments capable of containing and focusing this newfound magic. Large fragments known as Heart Stones, extremely powerful and dangerous, fell to Erasyl, altering reality itself and even causing new continents to rise around them.

Alarmed and enraged, the Didren captured an Adren named Gammis, determined to learn this secret of destruction. Under immense torment, Gammis eventually relented, revealing the method. The Didren tested it on him, confirming its devastating effectiveness. Realizing their newfound vulnerability, both factions spiraled into catastrophic warfare, their battles shattering realms and scattering fragments of their essence across Oravyn.

Eventually, desperate and dwindling, the Adren executed a final, costly strategy. They sacrificed most of themselves to imprison the Didren within their own realms of Mythara, creating eternal prisons from their very essences. Although successful, this left only a handful of weakened Adren surviving, unable to physically shape Erasyl but able to subtly influence its fate through whispers, visions, and subtle manipulations.

These surviving Adren became the first true gods worshiped by mortals, embodying virtues and ideals essential to mortal life and civilization:

Maldara: Goddess of affection, unity, friendship, marriage, and emotional bonds.

Jalar: God of ingenuity, builders, tools, and skilled labor; brother of Jaral.

Jaral: Keeper of knowledge, logic, insight, reason, and scholarly wisdom; brother of Jalar.

Orelian: God of trade, wealth, prosperity, and negotiation.

Sylador: Overseer of fate, history, and the passage of time.

Seren: Patron of creativity, inspiration, performers, artists, and poets.

Mahar: God of worldly pleasures, indulgence, luxury, and celebration.

Vaelia: Goddess of the heavens, winds, seas, natural harmony, weather, and tranquility.

Kaelos: God of charity, generosity, luck, fortune, travelers, and goodwill.

Valdaris: God of justice, peace, law, and moral order.

The imprisoned Didren became deities of darker domains, each sealed within their realms, forever plotting to break free and reclaim their lost power:

Kaerith: Cruelty, torture, pain, and vengeance.

Nykareth: Tricks, greed, deception, thieves, and cunning plots.

Xalrok: Chaos, destruction, ruin, and disorder.

Xalrik: Strict, oppressive order; absolute discipline and ruthless conformity.

Malicyn: Curses, malice, hexes, and dark magic.

Morvala: Murder, brutality, bloodshed, and mortal agony.

Jakora: Seduction, infidelity, betrayal, temptation, and corruption.

Zhaedria: Darkness, shadows, fear, nightmares, secrecy, and hidden evil.

Pestaris: Disease, pestilence, decay, illness, and corruption.

Gammorath: Madness, insanity, delusion, mental torment, and chaos.

Thus began the universe of Heartlands, shaped by the eternal echoes of these cosmic struggles, forever marked by the interplay between the remnants of Adren benevolence and the imprisoned malice of the Didren. Mortals, unknowingly inheriting the echoes of this great conflict, live in a world forever touched by magic, divine intervention, and the lingering threat of ancient, imprisoned powers yearning for freedom.

Though trapped, the Didren send mortal servants into Erasyl to spread chaos, undermining stability and attempting to break free.
